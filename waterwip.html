Water

How water moves off the Helderberg escarpment toward the Hudson, and what the public record says about its quality at each stop — and where that record goes quiet. Two transects from the Westerlo–Rensselaerville divide: north through Basic Creek and Alcove to Coeymans (Albany's drinking water), south through Ten Mile and Catskill Creek to Catskill. Empty nodes are a finding, not a gap to hide.

1 · The two transects — elevation on the vertical, ordered downstream; hollow means no data

Shape says what the node is (circle lake or reservoir · line stream · triangle groundwater · diamond public system · cross outfall); a diamond nested in a circle or triangle says what the system draws from; fill says how much we know (hollow none · thin ring thin · filled rich). Elevations marked ~ are estimates awaiting a USGS 3DEP lookup. Staleness is shown as a year, not a colour. Click a node for its card.

2 · Node cards — one badge, one headline number against its standard, then the rows behind it

⚠ impaired / violation◐ near standard✓ within standards○ no data⏱ stale = newest number older than 5 years

Finished water (at the tap, from Annual Water Quality Reports) and raw ambient water (the lake or stream itself, DEC portal) describe different water at different stages of treatment. Cards label which is which and never put the two on one axis. The headline is the newest year's value that sits closest to its standard (a violation always wins). A superscript d marks a number derived in the workbook rather than copied from the source; ~ marks an assumption meant to be edited.

3 · What the bugs say — DEC Biological Assessment Profile along each creek, 1984–2022

Macroinvertebrate sampling is the one method built to compare sites across decades: the same 100-organism kick sample, scored the same way, at stations named by river mile. The Biological Assessment Profile (BAP) averages four metrics onto one 0–10 scale and DEC reads it in four bands. Upstream is on the left, the mouth on the right; darker dots are more recent years; the ringed dot is each station's latest visit. Hover for the Nutrient Biotic Index (phosphorus), which reads enrichment straight off the organisms present.

4 · Basic Creek Reservoir — impaired for phosphorus (TMDL 2013) and dissolved oxygen (303d 2022); Albany's backup supply

5 · Groundwater — USGS ambient wells: the plateau's bedrock signature against the valley floor

Plateau bedrock wells within 15 km of the divide are soft sodium-bicarbonate water — calcium swapped for sodium on clay surfaces during a long stay in shale — with lithium, boron and radon along for the ride. Valley wells in alluvium that touches the creeks are the opposite water: iron, manganese, methane, road salt. Each dot is one well (its latest sample year); hover for the panel.

6 · Outfalls — each permitted discharge as a line against its limit, not a green or red dot

A limit is a threshold, and a threshold is blind to direction. Annual medians of the monthly discharge reports, with the year's maximum as a faint dot and the permit limit as the red line. EPA's FY2017–18 monthly extracts are empty (their defect); Loading Tool annual means bridge those years and are drawn hollow because they are a different clock. Pre-2019 EPA extracts carry no unit column, so a unit was assigned only where the limit fingerprint matches a later year; those rows are marked.

7 · Where it sits — catchment, valley-fill aquifers, karst, dams and outfalls

The Basic Creek Reservoir catchment (USGS StreamStats, 11,318 ac) runs north into Berne; both sewage plants sit outside it, below the dam, upstream of Alcove. Valley-fill sand-and-gravel aquifers (DEC 250K) hug the creek floors — every public intake sits on bedrock beside them. Karst sinkholes (USGS lidar) form a belt along the escarpment front 15–20 km north and east; the Westerlo divide is on shale above it. Dam rings: red = DEC "Unsound," amber = deficiently maintained, white = no deficiencies, dashed = not rated.
