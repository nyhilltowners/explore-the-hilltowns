### Policy context (captured, not register rows)
- **NFIP** created 1968 (before it, flood-zone owners couldn't get insurance, relied on federal relief); revised 1972 (mandatory for federally-regulated lenders in flood zones) and 1994 (lender penalties). **>$20B in debt by Sept 2011** after Irene/Lee — the driver behind the premium hikes.
- 2015 remap mechanics: newly-mapped Special Flood Hazard Area parcels must buy flood insurance or face rising premiums (**capped 20%/yr**); some grandfathering.
- **Flood insurance ≠ homeowners' insurance:** NFIP covers ground-level rising water / topographic events (incl. mudslides), *not* e.g. rain through a leaky roof — relevant when reading damage reports.

### NOT added — out of register scope
- **Tufa caves of Vanhornesville** (Otsquago Creek valley, Owen D. Young CSD Outdoor Learning Center, ~Herkimer/Otsego line, ~90 min W of Albany, ~47 mi from Westerlo — inside the footprint). These are **depositional** features — calcite laid *down* from calcium-carbonate-saturated spring water — the **opposite process** from the dissolution/collapse karst in the Subsidence & Karst sheet. Not a hazard, not an event, not karst subsidence → it does not belong in the register.   - **Home for it:** a natural-history / POI note for the **Hilltowns Folk Atlas / Lore Map** — a quirky, mappable public preserve, not climate data.

### Mechanism note (for the karst sheet's education, already partly captured)
Tufa forms when a karst-aquifer stream stays super-saturated / pressurized so it does **not** de-gas underground; on emerging to the surface the pressure drop (or mixing with plain water) de-gasses it and precipitates calcite on whatever's in the streambed — "petrified shaving cream," often with embedded twigs/leaf impressions. Cave-type taxonomy from the column: **solutional** (dissolution — the register's karst), **fracture**, **lava**, **talus**, and **tufa** (depositional). Only solutional = the register's Subsidence & Karst inventory.
