  Purpose
  The map shows how water moves off the Helderberg escarpment toward the Hudson, and
  what is known about its quality at each stop along the way. The reader is someone who
  lives in the Hilltowns and drinks from a well, a reservoir or a small water district, and wants
  to understand what the public record says about their water and where that record goes
  quiet.

  The map must not overclaim. Most nodes have one or two numbers from different years
  and different labs. It can show what has been measured, when, and against what
  standard; it cannot show a trend along the slope until the raw ambient series is in hand
  (section 9). Empty nodes are a finding, not a gap to hide: the headwaters that feed
  everything are the least sampled.


  The territory
  The Westerlo–Rensselaerville plateau is a drainage divide at 1,500–1,800 ft, so the map has
  two transects sharing one headwater, not one slope. North side: Basic Creek → Basic
  Creek Reservoir → Alcove Reservoir → Hannacroix Creek → Hudson at Coeymans; this is
  Albany's drinking water. South side: Myosotis Lake → Ten Mile Creek (Rensselaerville) →
  Catskill Creek past Greenville and Cairo → Hudson at Catskill. Potic Reservoir, which
  serves Catskill village, is a third small drainage that enters the Hudson at Coxsackie.

  The escarpment is Silurian–Devonian limestone (the Helderberg Group) over Ordovician
  shale, which is why it is New York's type karst: sinkholes, caves, and closed depressions
  where surface water drops straight into bedrock. Below the escarpment the valleys are
  cut in shale with glacial till and alluvium on top. Wells on the plateau draw from fractured
  limestone and shale; wells in the Cairo and Catskill valleys draw from alluvium that is in
  contact with the creeks.

  Source type sorts the water quality signal better than elevation does. Every surface-water
  supply (Rensselaerville, Catskill, Albany) fights disinfection byproducts, which form when
  chlorine meets dissolved organic carbon; neither groundwater supply (Greenville, Cairo)
  does. The groundwater supplies instead carry a bedrock signature: radium, barium,
  fluoride, iron and manganese. The layout should make that split visible before it makes
  elevation visible.

  Symbol grammar and layout rules
  Shape encodes what the node is; nesting encodes what it draws from; fill encodes how
  much we know.

       Circle = lake or reservoir. Line = stream. Triangle = groundwater (well or well field).
       Diamond = public water system.
       A diamond nested inside a circle or triangle says what that system draws from. Cairo's
       triangle is hatched: groundwater under the influence of surface water.
       Hollow symbol = no data. Westerlo's hollow triangle at the top of both transects is the
       first thing the map should say.




                                                                                                                       Page 5 of 17
Cartographer's Brief — Helderberg–Hudson Water




       Ring weight or opacity scales with data status: none, thin, rich. Staleness shows as a
       date label, not a color; Basic Creek's most recent lake phosphorus is from 2018 and the
       reader should see that.
       Two vertical columns sharing a headwater node at the top, ordered by downstream
       rank, with elevation on the y-axis. A third short column for Potic if it fits; otherwise a
       footnote node beside Catskill.
       Flip card: front = one badge (impaired, violation, clean, no data), one headline number
       with its percent of standard, and a June–November bloom strip for surface nodes.
       Back = the measurement rows grouped by parameter family (nutrient, disinfection
       byproduct, geogenic metal, PFAS, plumbing metal), each with sample date and source
       link.
       Any panel that mixes finished-water numbers with raw-source numbers labels the two
       differently. They describe different water at different stages of treatment and should
       never sit on the same axis.
       PFAS and phosphorus panels use log scales. The PFAS range in hand is 2 ng/L to 27,819
       ng/L; on a linear scale every local point disappears.
       Karst sinkholes from the lidar layer are a base-map layer along the escarpment front,
       drawn as small dots or stipple, never as symbols that compete with the water nodes.
       Confident outcrop sinkholes solid, covered-karst candidates hollow. The belt should
       read as a boundary between two ground types, with Westerlo visibly above it on the
       shale.

  Only the raw ambient layer can show change over time; it is now in hand for 21 local
  waterbodies (17,121 samples, DEC portal export 2026-09-28). Sleepy Hollow Lake, with
  phosphorus in 16 summers since 2004, is the regional control series; Thompsons Lake has
  seven summers 1994–2000 and resumed in 2025. Everything else is a snapshot or a
  judgment.


  Working theories
  Each is labeled with what supports it, what cuts against it, and what would settle it. None
  is established.

   1. Basic Creek Reservoir is improving but still blooms, and the mechanism is shallow-
       water anoxia, not deep stratification. DEC's own follow-up sampling (portal export,
       2026-09-28) shows summer-mean total phosphorus of 44 µg/L in 2004, 32 in 2013 and
       21 in 2018, against the 20 target; chlorophyll fell from 38 to 11 µg/L and Secchi depth
       doubled to 1.9 m. The watershed side of the TMDL appears to be working. But the
       reservoir is only 3.5–4 m deep and does not thermally stratify (surface and bottom
       within 1 °C), and bottom dissolved oxygen still fell to 0.04 mg/L (July 2013) and 0.91
       (Aug 2018), with manganese near 200 µg/L and iron 381 µg/L in the same events. In a
       shallow polymictic reservoir that is sediment oxygen demand under a warm, calm
       water column, and it recharges phosphorus from the bottom on any hot still week.
       Shoreline bloom samples carried microcystin of 83 µg/L (Sept 2013), 487 (Aug 2014)
       and 6,993 (Oct 2014), against a recreational guidance of 4. Against: three sampling
       years is a thin trend, and 2025's fifteen bloom reports came after 2018's improvement.
       Settled by: 2023 and 2026 lake sampling if it exists, and the 2025 bloom sample results.
   2. The reservoir is a five-week mirror of its watershed. Hydraulic residence time is 0.1
       years, so Basic Creek barely buffers what comes off the land. Wet years should show
       up as phosphorus spikes within the same season. Against: a fast-flushing reservoir also

Cartographer's Brief — Helderberg–Hudson Water




       clears quickly, which could mask internal loading. Settled by: joining the raw ambient
       series to the regional climate event register by year.
   3. Thirty percent of the phosphorus arrives underground, but not through karst. The
      TMDL attributes 616 lbs/yr to groundwater transport, two-thirds from agriculture. The
      lidar karst layer (USGS SIR 2021-5094; 40 verified outcrop sinkholes, 14 covered-karst
       candidates) shows the soluble limestone belt runs along the escarpment front 15–20
       km north and east of Westerlo, from Knox through Berne, New Scotland and Clarksville
       to Coeymans — the Onesquethaw, Vloman Kill and Coeymans Creek drainages, on the
       Albany side. Nothing lies within 12 km of the Westerlo divide, the septage site, the
       anomalous well or Basic Creek Reservoir; the nearest outcrop sinkhole is 16 km away.
       The Basic Creek watershed sits on Hamilton Group shale above the carbonate, so its
       groundwater fraction moves as fractured-shale and till interflow, slower and more
       diffuse than sinkhole recharge. That is consistent with the plateau wells' sodium-
       bicarbonate chemistry, which takes long residence in shale to develop. Against:
       covered-karst collapse is possible wherever Onondaga is buried under Hamilton, and
       three candidates sit 12–14 km east toward Coeymans. Settled: karst is a real story for
       the Thacher Park escarpment and the Albany-side creeks, not for the Westerlo divide.
       Map it as its own belt, not as the plateau's recharge mechanism.
   4. Same carbon, two rulebooks. The vegetation and nutrient loading that shows up as
       phosphorus and blooms at the top of the hill shows up as trihalomethanes at every
       surface-water tap below it. Rensselaerville hit 84 µg/L in one quarter; Catskill violated
       the 80 µg/L running average in 2022; Albany runs 43–59 every year from 2019 to 2025
       with finished-water TOC of 1.6–1.9 mg/L, and Basic Creek Reservoir's raw DOC is 3–6
       mg/L. Against: TTHM also depends on chlorine dose and contact time, which are
       treatment choices, and Albany draws from Alcove, which is deep and clear, not from
       Basic Creek. Settled by: raw-water TOC at each intake, which the AWQRs do not
       report; ask the operators.
   5. Groundwater picks up geology going down and picks up us going toward the valley
      floor. The USGS groundwater layer (193 wells in the box, 10 on the plateau; WQP export
       2026-09-28) now shows the split directly. Plateau bedrock wells within 15 km of the
       divide, sampled 2008–2021, are soft sodium-bicarbonate water: pH 7.9–9.0, sodium 109–
       139 mg/L against chloride of 2–6, hardness 30–40, lithium 220–448 µg/L, boron 330–640,
       arsenic to 5.9. That chemistry is cation exchange in shale: calcium swapped for sodium
       on clay surfaces as water sits in the rock for a long time. The nearest well (3.6 km,
       2008) is the exception, with chloride 62 and sodium 109, which is road salt riding on the
       same base. The Cairo valley well (2018) is the opposite water: iron 879, manganese 744,
       methane detected, chloride 82, a reducing alluvial aquifer in contact with the creek.

       Radon is the plateau's other signature: 1,830 pCi/L in a Rensselaerville-area well (2013),
       900 at Cairo, 360–460 elsewhere, against a proposed 300 MCL. Against: one sample
       per well, most from a single year; the 1992 cluster near Oak Hill with chloride to 13,000
       mg/L is a salt-storage study, not ambient water. Settled by: the summer 2026 USGS
       resample, and a plateau private-well test that reports pH, sodium, hardness, lithium
       and radon.
   6. Cairo's PFOS came from somewhere nearby. At Cannon AFB, PFHxS out-runs PFOS
      along the flow path, so a downgradient well is PFHxS-dominant. Cairo's finished water
       is PFOS-dominant (6.75 ng/L) with PFHxS at 2.5, which on that logic suggests a short
       travel distance. Against: one sample; different geology; AFFF fingerprints differ from
       other PFAS sources. Settled by: a full PFAS panel with precursor assay at the two Cairo
       wells, or a DEC source investigation if one exists.
   7. Westerlo's silence is structural, not accidental. No public system beyond a small
      Westerlo Southside Water Coop that surfaced in ECHO and is not yet confirmed with
      the town, no active USGS well, no ambient-network site. The state monitors where
       people are served, and the plateau serves no one but itself. Against: the USGS ambient
       network has sampled ten plateau wells since 2008, so the plateau is not unmonitored,
       only unmonitored by the public-supply rules. What changes it: a certified private-well
       test on the divide, compared against those ten.
   8. The two sewage plants on Basic Creek are Alcove's problem, not the TMDL's.
      Resolved 2026-09-29. A StreamStats catchment delineated at the Basic Creek Dam
      (11,318 acres; the TMDL's 10,522 plus the reservoir and a sliver below the dam) extends
       north into Berne, not south into Greenville: Basic Creek flows south out of the
       reservoir, through Greenville, to Alcove Reservoir. Greenville (T) WWTP (NY0094854,
       0.02 MGD, 7 effluent exceedances) and Camp Malka (NY0269093, 0.027 MGD
       seasonal, 22 exceedances over 8 non-compliant quarters) discharge to Basic Creek
       tributaries 4–5 km outside that boundary, below the dam. So the TMDL's zero
       wasteload allocation is correct for Basic Creek Reservoir, and the earlier draft of this
       theory was wrong. What survives is sharper: both plants sit upstream of Alcove,
       Albany's primary supply, which has no TMDL, no phosphorus limit on either permit, and
       an oligotrophic assessment that rests on a 32.5 sq mi catchment diluting whatever
       arrives. The TMDL's own weaknesses stand on their own: built with no observed
       phosphorus data (validated afterward on five samples each in 2004 and 2005, which
       came in at 42 and 55 µg/L against a simulated 31), and internal loading from anoxic
       sediment left at zero despite DEC's 2013 and 2018 profiles showing bottom anoxia.
       Only four dams sit inside the catchment (Basic Creek, Camp Givah, Duker Pond,

       Snyder Farm Pond); no USGS well and no land-application site does. Draw the two
       plants as point-source glyphs on Basic Creek between the two reservoirs, pointed at
       Alcove.
   9. The valley-fill aquifers are the corridors; everything we monitor sits on bedrock. The
       DEC Unconsolidated Aquifers layer (907 polygons, now point-in-polygon tagged for
       every node, well, dam and facility in the workbook) maps sand-and-gravel aquifers only
       along the valley floors: Basic Creek below the reservoir, Eightmile and Ten Mile creeks,
       Catskill Creek from Greenville down. Every public intake on both transects, the
       reference point, the USGS plateau wells and 18 of 19 nodes fall outside them; the one
       exception is Camp Malka's plant, which sits on a 10–100 gpm valley-fill unit. Read with
       theory 5, this says the sodium-bicarbonate signature is the bedrock-well norm here
       and the valley aquifers, where road salt, septic and fertilizer arrive fastest, are the
       places the state does not have a public well to watch. Against: the 250K layer is coarse
       and 1980s-vintage; a bedrock well drilled through thin valley fill is still tagged "valley-
       fill." Draw the aquifer polygons as a pale underlay so the hollow nodes read as sitting on
       rock beside them.
 10. Compliance hides trend. Eighteen years of discharge reports show movements that
     no violation ever flagged: Haleon Oak Hill's phosphorus median rising tenfold since
       2023 while staying under a generous limit; SABIC's phosphate falling by two-thirds
       since 2007 (2,270 → 760 kg/yr) with no one to credit; CSX Selkirk's phosphorus
       climbing (205 → 384 kg/yr) as its ammonia fell to zero; Greenville's influent taking 1,600
       mg/L BOD slugs a small town cannot generate; Berne WWTP's load quadrupling in nine
       years. A limit is a threshold, and a threshold is blind to direction. The dashboard should
       show each outfall as a sparkline against its limit, not as a green or red dot. Against:
       annual Loading Tool figures carry an estimation factor when months are missing, and
       pre-2009 reporting is patchy; treat any single-year jump as unverified until the monthly
       rows agree.


  Stories worth telling, and stories to refuse
  In priority order, with what each needs.

   1. The monitoring shadow. Where the state looks and where it doesn't. Fully supported
       now; the hollow nodes at the top of both transects carry it. Lead with this. Note the
       elevation order the 3DEP values give: Myosotis 1,536 → Rensselaerville intake 1,500 →
       Onderdonk 1,392 → Warners 1,199 → Basic Creek Reservoir 928 → Greenville wells 697 →
       Alcove 609 → Potic 424 → Cairo wells 370.

   2. Same carbon, two rulebooks. Phosphorus and blooms above, disinfection byproducts
       below, sorted by source type rather than elevation. Fully supported by the AWQRs and
       the DEC factsheet.
   3. Bedrock signature versus human signature. Greenville against Cairo. Supported as a
       contrast of two nodes; becomes a gradient only when the Lower Hudson groundwater
       release is in hand.
   4. The five-week reservoir. Basic Creek against the climate event register. Needs the raw
      ambient series; this is the story the dashboard should be built toward.
   5. Where the water goes underground. The karst belt along the escarpment front
      against the shale plateau above it — two recharge regimes side by side. Fully
      supported now by the lidar sinkhole layer; the surprise is that Westerlo is on the shale
       side.
   6. The dam that holds the impaired water is rated unsound. Basic Creek Dam (1928, high
      hazard, 3,922 ac-ft) was inspected 2024-04-09 and carries DEC's "Unsound –
       Deficiency Recognized" rating; Alcove Dam below it has no deficiencies. Myosotis,
       Potic, Onderdonk and Warners lake dams are all "Unsound" or "Deficiently maintained."
       A condition-coded ring on each impoundment glyph tells this without a word. Needs
       the inspection reports themselves before saying what the deficiency is.

  Refuse, for now:

       Any claim of a downslope trend in a single parameter. With one or two values per site
       from different years and labs, a gradient plot would look convincing and mean nothing.
       Any statement about private-well safety in Westerlo. There is no data; the map says so.
       Any comparison of finished-water numbers to raw-water numbers on one axis.
       Any implication that the Cannon, Cape Cod or New Jersey studies describe local
       conditions. They are exemplars of process, and the map labels them as such.

  Handoff package (2026-09-29)
  Everything the dashboard needs is in one zip, helderberg_hudson_package_2026-09-
  29.zip . Its README repeats this section with file-level detail.

  Data model. Three keys join everything. site_id (the sites sheet, 21 nodes) is the node
  key for measurements, assessments, HABs, TMDL and profiles. permit (NPDES ID, e.g.
  NY0094854) is the key for every discharge table: echo_cwa_permits → dmr_limits /
  dmr_results / dmr_summary / dmr_annual / loading_tool_pollutant . sid (USGS site
  number) keys the groundwater tables. Every table with coordinates carries km_from_ref
  (distance from Rensselaerville hamlet), aquifer_setting (valley-fill vs bedrock),
   dec_basin , and in_basic_creek_res_catchment where relevant, so a layer can be
  filtered without a spatial join.

  What to draw, in priority. (1) The two transects as ordered node strips, elevation on the
  vertical, sites.downstream_rank for order, with the symbol grammar above and hollow
  glyphs where data_status is thin or none. (2) The catchment polygon and the valley-fill
  aquifer underlay, so hollow nodes read as sitting on rock beside the corridors the state
  does not watch. (3) Outfalls as sparklines against their limit from dmr_annual (effluent
  rows only), not as pass/fail dots — theory 10. (4) Impoundments with a condition ring from
   ny_dams.LastConditionRating . (5) The reference-study markers from
   pfas_map_studies , clearly labelled as exemplars of process, with Cannon's 42 exact
  sites as the one drillable study.

  Cadence and comparability. Finished-water (annual, at the tap), raw ambient (DEC, years
  apart), continuous DMR (monthly, at the pipe) and EPA annual loads are four different
  clocks. Never put two on one axis without saying so; the layer column in the data-layers
  table is the guide. Concentrations (mg/L) and loads (kg/yr) are different quantities;
   dmr_annual.unit and loading_tool_kg_yr keep them apart.

  Known holes. FY2017–18 monthly DMRs (EPA extract defect; annual loads bridge it). Basic
  Creek Reservoir water-column data after 2018. Ten of 21 node elevations still estimates
  (3DEP timed out). Study centroids for Cape Cod, NAWC and Darby Creek are ±0.5–1 km.
  AA/AA(s) watersheds and wetlands have no geometry yet. Albany AWQR 2022 and 2024
  not obtained.

  Provenance rule. Blue cells are verbatim from the named source; black are derived; yellow
  are assumptions meant to be edited. Every sheet's A1 comment says where it came from
  and what was done to it; the sources and references sheets are the bibliography. If a
