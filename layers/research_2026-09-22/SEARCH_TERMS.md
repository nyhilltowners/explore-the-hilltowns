# Period search vocabulary — finding geohazards & weather in 1800s–early-1900s papers

A crib for searching the Argus, Altamont Enterprise, Cohoes Republican, Ravena News-Herald, Catskill Mountain News, etc. The key trap: **modern hazard words often didn't exist yet** — 19th-c. writers used older words or just described the damage. Search the period word, not the modern one.

---

## FLOODS
- **freshet** ← the #1 term (a spring/rain river flood, often snowmelt-driven)
- high water · "the river rose" · overflow · inundation · deluge · "the flood"
- **cloudburst** (a sudden torrential downpour)
- **ice gorge / ice jam / ice freshet** (ice-dam flooding — check late-winter/early-spring issues)
- backwater · "swollen streams/creek" · "burst its banks" · washout (rain-cut)
- Damage phrasings: "cellars flooded" · "docks under water" · "tracks submerged"

## LANDSLIDES / SLOPE FAILURES
- **landslide · landslip · slide** · "a slide"
- "the bank gave way / caved in" · **cave-in** · "a caving bank"
- **"an avalanche of earth/mud"** (avalanche was used for earth, not just snow)
- "the clay bank slid" · slump · "earth slid" · "embankment gave way" · washout (rain-induced)
- **blue clay / yellow clay** (see note below — these two words find clay-bank slides *and* the brickyards)

## SINKHOLES / KARST / SUBSIDENCE
- **sink hole / sink-hole · sink** · "a sink"
- "the ground gave way / opened / caved in" · cave-in · "a hole opened"
- **swallow hole** (older British-influenced term) · "natural well" · chasm · subsidence · "settling"
- "the earth swallowed"

## CAVES
- **cave · cavern · grotto** · "natural bridge" · fissure · **subterranean** · "explored a cave"
- (Note: in the Catskills interior a "cave" is usually a *talus/crevice* cave, not a limestone one — see geology note. The real solution caves are the Helderberg belt: Howe, Secret, Schoharie, McFail's, Knox, Onesquethaw, Clarksville, Barrack Zourie, Gage, Skull, Tory.)

## MAJOR STORMS / WIND
- **cyclone** ← the everyday 19th-c. word for a **tornado** OR a violent local windstorm ("a cyclone struck"). Search this FIRST for twisters.
- **tornado** (used, but rarer than "cyclone" for local twisters)
- **gale · windstorm / wind storm · hurricane** ("a perfect hurricane," "hurricane of wind" = any severe wind, not a tropical system)
- **whirlwind · wind spout · squall** · "the elements" · "the storm king" · tempest · "a terrific wind"
- Damage keywords that find wind events fast: **"unroofed" · "blown down" · "leveled" · "twisted off" · "swept"**
- Thunderstorms: **thunder storm · electrical storm · lightning · "struck by lightning" · a bolt**
- ⚠ **No period word for a microburst / downburst / derecho.** "Microburst" was coined in the 1970s; "derecho" (1888) almost never appears in papers. A downburst was reported as a **cyclone, whirlwind, wind spout, squall,** or "a sudden gust," and identified by the *damage pattern* — a narrow path, trees twisted off, a building unroofed. So: search **cyclone / whirlwind / "unroofed"** and read for a localized, short-lived, straight-line-ish path.

## BLIZZARDS / SNOW
- ⚠ **"blizzard" is anachronistic before ~the late 1870s** (it caught on nationally after the March 1888 storm). For earlier snow events search: **"snow storm" · "great storm" · "snow blockade" · "the storm" · drifts · "snow bound" · sleet**
- After ~1888: **blizzard** works well.

## FREEZES / COLD WAVES
- **cold wave · cold snap** · "the mercury fell/dropped" · "zero weather" · "arctic wave" · "the cold term"
- **"hard freeze / sudden freeze"** · "the river closed" · "ice bridge" · "the Hudson frozen over" (river freeze-up)
- **killing frost · black frost · hard frost** (agricultural cold-damage events)

## HEAT / DROUGHT
- **heated term · hot wave / heat wave · the drought · dry spell** · "prostrated by the heat" · sunstroke (a proxy for heat casualties)

## Note — "blue clay" and "yellow clay"
Both are the **Glacial Lake Albany varved clay** (an 1850s geologic name for it was literally the **"Albany clay"**) — a glacial-lake-bottom (glaciolacustrine) deposit laid down ~15,000–12,600 years ago when the Hudson Valley was a long proglacial lake dammed by ice. It forms the bluffs and river-hills on both sides of the Hudson from Albany north through the Champlain Valley, 6–150 ft thick.
- **Blue clay** = the deep, *unweathered / unoxidized (reduced)* clay — blue-gray because its iron is in the reduced state below the water table. Illite-rich and water-rich, it is the **sensitive ("quick") clay** that fails in rapid retrogressive earth-flows — the mechanism of the Normanskill / Hudson clay-bank slides (and the 1993 Tully Valley slide near Syracuse). It was also the prized **brick clay** dug by hand from the river banks (hence the brickyards — Coeymans, Haverstraw, the Bartlett/Mica-works sites).
- **Yellow clay** = the *same* clay near the surface, weathered and **oxidized**, so its iron has rusted to yellow-brown/tan — a stiffer crust over the blue clay.
So "the yellow clay slid off the blue clay," or "the bank of blue clay gave way," is a report of a failure in the Lake Albany clay — the register's core landslide mechanism. Searching **"blue clay"** alone is a strong way to surface both slides and brickyard items.

*Handy local secondary source for all of the above: the Altamont Enterprise's "Back Roads Geology" column (already one of our sources).*
