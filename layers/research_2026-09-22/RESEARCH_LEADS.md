## A. Historic-site coordinate hunts (pin candidates — locus named, coordinates not fixed)

These three are staged as Events with `CHECK` flags; each deserves a ⛰️ Geology pin, but the loci are historic industrial sites that don't resolve in a modern map search. Fix the coordinate and each becomes a pin.

**1. Bartlett brickyard clay-bank slide (1890)** — *Events row 317.*
- Known: the **William E. Bartlett brickyard, ~1 mi north of Hudson** (Columbia Co.), destroyed Sep 21 1890 when ~2,000 tons of blue clay slid from the bank. Part of the Hudson glaciolacustrine clay-bank slide family.
- Find: the brickyard's riverfront location (long gone).
- Look: **Sanborn fire-insurance maps of Hudson** (Library of Congress, digitized); **USGS historical topos via topoView** (ngmdb.usgs.gov/topoview) for the 1890s–1900s Hudson riverfront brickyard belt; the **Columbia County Historical Society** (Kinderhook) and the well-documented **Hudson–Athens brickmaking district** histories. The Bartlett name should place it.

**2. "Iron Works station" recurring clay slide (1902)** — *Events row 320.*
- Known: an **annually recurring** clay slide on the **Central-Hudson (NYC) Troy & Rensselaer branch near "Iron Works station"** (Rensselaer Co.), a point that "menaced the railroad for several years." Yellow clay; retaining walls gave little resistance.
- Find: where "Iron Works station" was on that branch.
- Look: **ICC/NYS railroad valuation maps** for the Troy & Rensselaer branch (valuation sections show every station and cut); **USGS historical topos** of the Troy–Rensselaer riverfront; local Rensselaer Co. rail histories. The recurrence makes this the best pin of the three — its Notes can accumulate the annual slides.

**3. Mica Insulating Works slide (1903)** — *Events row 322.*
- Known: a landslide in **western Schenectady** buried two buildings of the **Mica Insulating Works** under a cliff-fall on Jan 30 1903 (~$25,000 loss, ~25 out of work).
- Find: the works' location in western Schenectady.
- Look: **Sanborn maps of Schenectady** (early 1900s — the firm should be labeled); the **Schenectady County Historical Society / Grems-Doolittle Library**; city directories of the era. "Partly overhanging cliff" suggests a specific bluff in the west end.
